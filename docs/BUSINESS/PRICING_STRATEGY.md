# VERCODE — Pricing Strategy

The commercial model. Pricing is a *cost model* decision (`OPERATIONS/COST_MODEL.md` — the margin) + a *positioning* decision (`PRODUCT/PRODUCT_VISION.md` — the evidence-backed, cost-disciplined second-pair-of-eyes) + a *market* decision (the INR target, `ADR-009`). This document is the *plan ladder*; the *source of truth* for the quota numbers is `packages/contracts/src/plans.ts` (AC-401 — the API quota check, the worker pre-flight, and the dashboard all import from there; a *hardcoded* quota number elsewhere is a defect).

## 1. The principle (what we price, and what we don't)

- **We price the *capability*, not the *seats*.** A plan is a *review capacity* (reviews/month + a token-spend cap + a model tier), not a per-seat license (a team that opens PRs is *billed on the reviews*, not the heads — a *usage* model, `USAGE_QUOTAS.md`). A *seat* model would *punish* a team that *adopts it* more deeply (the *more* a team uses it, the *more* they pay *per head*; a *usage* model aligns the *bill* with the *value* (a team that gets *more* findings-acted-on pays for the *reviews that produced them*, the *north-star metric*, `SUCCESS_METRICS.md`).
- **The *Free* tier is a *real* product, not a trial** (a *permanent* Free org, the *activation* funnel, `PRODUCT/MVP_SCOPE.md` "a user signs in → creates an org → installs the App → gets a review" — a *Free* tier that *works* (a review *is published*, a finding *is actionable*) is the *activation*; a *Free* tier that's a *time-boxed trial* is a *leak* (a trial that expires before activation churns, `FEATURE_ROADMAP`'s "Free→paid conversion ≥ 8% in 60 d").
- **The *Enterprise* tier is *cost-shifting*, not just *more*.** The Enterprise play is *BYOK* (the *customer's* provider key, a *$0* marginal cost to *us*, `MODEL_ROUTING §6`, `COST_MODEL §3`) + *Ollama* (a *self-hosted* model, a *zero marginal cost*) + *frontier-model* access + *SSO/SCIM* + *data-residency* (a *compliance* play, `COMPLIANCE_CONSIDERATIONS`). An *Enterprise* org is a *high-revenue + low-COGS* account (a *BYOK/Ollama* Enterprise is a *near-zero* COGS, a *high-margin* account — the *margin* is the *Enterprise* story, `COST_MODEL §3` "the Enterprise margin is the highest").

## 2. The plan ladder (the numbers live in `plans.ts`; this is the *shape*)

| Plan | Positioning | The capability it sells | The cost profile (`COST_MODEL.md`) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Free** | the *activation* funnel; a *permanent* Free org | 50 reviews/mo; 20 files/review; 1 concurrent; 30 d finding retention; 3 custom rules; the *standard* (cheap) model tier; a *cheap-triage-gated* cost profile | a *~$0.02–0.05/review* COGS (the *cheap model only*, *never* the strong model on a Free tier — a Free plan that runs a frontier model is a *negative-margin* plan, `COST_MODEL §2` "a Free tier must not be a frontier-model plan") |
| **Developer** | an *individual* / a *small team's* paid tier; the *first* paid step | 500 reviews/mo; 60 files/review; 3 concurrent; 90 d retention; 10 rules; the *standard* tier; the *cheap-triage* cost control | a *~$0.05–0.15/review* COGS (a *higher volume* at the *same* per-review cost as Free — the *margin* is the *volume*, not the *model*) |
| **Team** | the *core* commercial tier; a *team* that wants the *strong* model | 5,000 reviews/mo; 100 files/review; 10 concurrent; 1 y retention; 50 rules; the *standard + strong* tier access (a *strong* deep + security pass, `MODEL_ROUTING §4`); the *cost/review* rises (a strong model, not just a cheap one) | a *~$0.15–0.35/review* COGS (a *stronger* model, a *higher* per-review cost — the *price* is *much* higher than the Developer, the *margin* comes from the *price*, `COST_MODEL §3` "a Team plan sells the strong model; the cost is higher but the price is much higher") |
| **Enterprise** | the *cost-shifting* + the *compliance* + the *frontier* play | *unlimited-ish* caps (a *configurable*, a *negotiated* level, `plans.ts`); the *frontier* model access; *BYOK* + *Ollama* (a *$0 / a customer-self-funded* COGS, `MODEL_ROUTING §6`); *SSO/SCIM*; *data-residency*; a *custom* retention; a *platform-cap* carve-out (the `PLATFORM_TOKEN_SPEND_CAP_USD` is *per-plan* on Enterprise, a *negotiated* ceiling) | a *near-zero* COGS on a *BYOK/Ollama* account (a *customer*-funded provider bill, a *customer*-self-hosted model); a *frontier-model* COGS on a *platform-funded* account (a *high-revenue + high-COGS* account, a *negotiated* margin) |

**The *ladder's* logic (a *step* at each rung, not a *cliff*):** Free → Developer is a *volume* step (the *same* cheap-model cost, *more* of it). Developer → Team is a *capability* step (a *stronger* model, a *higher* cost + a *much* higher price). Team → Enterprise is a *cost-shifting + compliance* step (a *BYOK/Ollama* a *$0* COGS, a *frontier* + a *residency* a *premium*). Each rung is a *distinct* value, not a *10× the previous* (a *pricing* that's *linear in the volume* is a *seat* model in *disguise*; a *capability* ladder is *step-wise*).

## 3. The *metered* overage (the "a plan cap that's *graceful*", `NFR-18`)

A plan's *cap* (the reviews/month + the token-spend cap) is a *hard* cap *by default* (a *skip-with-notice*, NFR-18 — an *over-cap* review is *skipped* + *posted*, *never* a *silent* over-bill, `RATE_LIMITING.md §5` "a plan can have an infinite quota but still hit the org RPS limit"). An *optional* *metered overage* (a "keep reviewing past your cap, billed per-review at a *published* rate") is a *Team/Enterprise* feature (a *Free/Developer* is a *hard* cap — a *Free* org that's *metered-overaged* is a *negative-margin* surprise, the *Free* tier is *not* a *revenue* tier, it's the *activation* funnel; a *Team/Enterprise* org that *wants* the *overage* opts *in* + *sees the rate* + a *cap on the overage* (a *bounded* overage, a *cost-abuse* backstop, `RATE_LIMITING §6`)). The *overage* is *priced* *transparently* (a *published* per-review overage rate, a *dashboard* "you'll be billed $X for N over-cap reviews this month" — a *cost-abuse* that's *surfaced*, not *hidden*).

## 4. The *annual* discount + the *commit* (the *Enterprise* shape)

- **An *annual* commit** (a *Team/Enterprise* *annual* prepay, a *~15–20%* discount for the *commit* — a *commit* is *revenue- certainty* + a *churn-reducer*, `SUCCESS_METRICS`'s "NRR ≥ 100% by month 6" *improved* by a *commit*).
- **An *Enterprise* *platform-cap* carve-out** (a *negotiated* `PLATFORM_TOKEN_SPEND_CAP_USD` per *Enterprise* org, a *cost-abuse* backstop that's *negotiated* to the *org's* *risk-appetite*, `RATE_LIMITING §6` "the platform cap is the backstop" — an *Enterprise* org that's *trusted* gets a *higher* cap, a *Free/Team* org gets the *default*).

## 5. The *INR* market + the *billing provider* (`ADR-009`, `BILLING_SPECIFICATION.md`)

- **The *market*:** a *developing-market* pricing (an *INR* price point, a *~5–10×* lower *per-review* cost than a *USD* Team plan — a *developing-market* discount, a *cost* that's *lower* because the *model* is the *same* but the *price* is *market-adjusted*, a *willingness-to-pay* decision, `SAAS_LAUNCH_PLAN`).
- **The *provider*:** the *INR* market *needs* a *local* billing provider (a *Razorpay/Rajatko* for *UPI + INR cards*; Stripe *does not* natively *acquire* *India-incorporated* *INR/UPI* — a *Stripe* is for the *global-card* + the *state-machine* pattern, `ADR-009` "Stripe for the billing state machine + provider-webhook pattern; a Razorpay adapter for the INR-market"). The *provider* is a *swappable* leaf (the `packages/billing` *adapter* boundary, `INTEGRATION_DESIGN §2` — a *Stripe* default + a *Razorpay* *INR* adapter, a *documented* deviation, `TECH_STACK` DEV-1).
- **The *eligibility* (a *verify-with-the-provider* item, `ADR-009` "Stripe's India-incorporated entity status for INR/UPI is the open question"):** a *production* INR deployment *uses* the *Razorpay* adapter (a *UPI + INR* *acquisition*, a *local* *compliance*, `COMPLIANCE_CONSIDERATIONS`'s "the DPDP alignment" — a *Razorpay* keeps the *payment data* *India-local*, a *DPDP* + a *residency* alignment).

## 6. The *free-tier* *as a *strategic* choice (not a *trial*)

A *permanent* Free tier (a *50 reviews/mo*) is a *strategic* cost (a *COGS* we *incur* for a *Free* org, a *~$0.02–0.05/review × 50 = a *~$1–2.50/mo* *COGS* per *Free* org). The *ROI* of a *Free* tier is the *activation + the *conversion* (a *Free → Team* *conversion* at a *~$49/mo* Team price *pays back* the *~$2/mo* Free COGS in a *~3-week* payback, a *conversion* that *funds* the *Free* tier). The *Free* tier's *cost* is *bounded* (a *50-review* cap + a *cheap-model-only* routing + a *20-file* cap, `plans.ts` — a *Free* org *cannot* run a *frontier model* or a *huge PR*, the *routing* is the *cost control*, `COST_MODEL §2`). A *Free* tier that's *unbounded* or *frontier-funded* is a *negative-margin* *liability* (the *routing + the cap* are the *two* things that *make a Free tier* *affordable*, `COST_MODEL §2`).

## 7. The *pricing decisions* that are *NOT* in this doc (the *documented* deferrals)

- **The *exact* price points** (a *Free* $0, a *Developer* $X, a *Team* $Y, an *Enterprise* custom) are a *market-research* + a *willingness-to-pay* decision (a *SAAS_LAUNCH_PLAN* input, a *competitor* analysis — a *pricing* that's *not* *market-validated* is a *guess*; the *structure* is here, the *numbers* are a *go-to-market* decision, `SAAS_LAUNCH_PLAN.md`).
- **The *overage* *rate* + the *annual* *discount %* (a *negotiated* + a *market-set* number, a *BILLING_SPECIFICATION* detail, the *mechanics* of a *metered overage* are in `BILLING_SPECIFICATION.md`, the *price* of it is a *market* decision).
- **The *Enterprise* *platform-cap* *negotiation* (a *per-account* *negotiated* ceiling, a *sales* decision, the *mechanics* are in `RATE_LIMITING §6`, the *number* is a *per-account* *commercial* call).

## 8. The *cost-revenue* *alignment* (the *unit economics*, `COST_MODEL §7`)

A *plan* is *profitable* iff `plan price > (plan's review quota × the plan's per-review cost) + the plan's fixed infra share` (a *margin* test, `COST_MODEL §3`). The *pricing* here is *structured* so that the *test* is *passable* at each rung (a *Free* is a *loss-leader* [a *documented* strategic COGS, a *conversion* *funnel*], a *Developer* is a *thin-margin* [a *volume* play], a *Team* is a *healthy-margin* [a *capability* play], an *Enterprise* is a *high-margin* [a *cost-shifting* + a *compliance* play]). The *measured* *cost/review* (the *BENCHMARK_DATASET* + the *production* `usage_events`, `AI_EVALUATION §5`) is what *validates* the *margin* at each rung (a *plan* whose *measured* cost/review *exceeds* its *price* is a *negative-margin* plan — a *pricing* *correction*, not a *cost* *correction*, `COST_MODEL §8` "a claim cites both the model + the measurement").
