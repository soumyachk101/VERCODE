The quota skip is a feature: cost discipline without a silent stop

NFR-18 makes the over-quota review skip with a posted notice, never a silent bill. The checkQuota() mechanism, the 80% banner, and the Free tier.

The claim

When an org hits its review cap, the right behavior is a visible stop with a notice — not a review that runs and bills anyway, and not a silent drop. NFR-18 in the product spec states the rule in one line: "skip-with-notice, never a silent stop". The trust argument is the product argument: a team that gets a posted notice at the cap can plan an upgrade; a team that gets a surprise over-bill learns to distrust the billing line, and the upgrade becomes a repair, not a choice. A silent stop is worse than no stop — it is a stop you did not know you had.

The mechanism: checkQuota() as one gate

The quota check is a worker pre-flight, not a billing-system afterthought. From docs/BUSINESS/USAGE_QUOTAS.md §4, the check reads the plan's quota from packages/contracts/src/plans.ts — the single source of truth, so the API, the worker, and the dashboard all read the same numbers and only the worker enforces them — and the org's usage aggregate for the month:

checkQuota(org, month) ->
  reviewsUsedThisMonth = aggregate(usage_events, org, month).review_count
  tokenSpendThisMonth  = aggregate(usage_events, org, month).est_usd_cost
  quota = planQuota(org.plan)
  if reviewsUsedThisMonth >= quota.reviewsPerMonth    -> "skip: quota_exceeded" (posted notice)
  if tokenSpendThisMonth >= quota.tokenSpendCapUsd   -> "skip: quota_exceeded" (posted notice)
  else -> proceed (the pipeline runs; a cost is recorded to usage_events)

The skip happens before the AI stage, which means a skipped review consumes zero model tokens — a quota stoppage is a cost control, not a cost. A skipped review that still burns tokens is a documented abuse defect, not a rounding error. The 50th review on a 50-review Free plan runs; the 51st skips.

The skip is recorded in three places, and that is the invariant. The job lands in the database with status skipped and and skip_reason quota_exceeded; the pull request gets the notice — "Quota reached this period. Upgrade or reduce review volume." — and the dashboard shows the row. docs/TECHNICAL/ERROR_HANDLING.md's "never silent" rule makes the three records a single check: a review that "just didn't happen" without a stored reason is a defect a nightly reconciliation job is built to catch.; the pull request gets the notice — "Quota reached this period. The 80% banner: the warning before the stop

The skip is the hard edge; the warning is the human one. The plan's quota strip in the dashboard (docs/DESIGN/PAGE_SPECIFICATIONS.md §5) carries an 80% banner — "Quota 80% — you've used 4,000 of 5,000 reviews this month" — above the usage track, so the org sees the approaching cap before it is reached, with the queued reviews drawn as a lighter committed segment so "4,000 used + 300 queued" reads as "you are about to hit the cap". The banner, the posted notice, and the skipped-review row are the whole "you will not be surprised" design: the warning is at 80%, the stop is at 100%, and both are visible in the workspace, not only in a billing email.

The Free tier as a strategic cost

The pricing strategy treats the Free tier as a deliberate cost, not a perk. docs/BUSINESS/PRICING_STRATEGY.md §6 prices it at a ~$1–2.50/month COGS per Free org: 50 reviews at a $0.02–0.05/review cheap-model cost, and never a frontier model on a Free tier. Against that sits a ~$49/month Team price with a ~3-week payback on conversion.

The skip is what makes the math bounded. The reviews-per-month, files-per-review, and token-spend ceilings hold the COGS, and the skip-with-notice stops a runaway Free org from quietly becoming a loss while it is still free. A Free tier that is unbounded, or that over-bills silently at the cap, is a negative-margin liability; one that degrades visibly at the cap — notice posted, tokens unspent, upgrade path one click away — is a funded funnel.

What this does not claim

The skip rule has edges that matter, and they are documented: an in-flight review finishes even when the cap is reached mid-run, because a skip is a pre-flight decision, not a mid-run one; a mid-month upgrade grants the new cap without re-billing the used reviews; and a giant single PR that would breach the token-spend cap skips rather than partially publishes, because a partial that exceeds the cap is itself an abuse. The numbers in plans.ts are the source; a quota hardcoded in a second place is a build-time defect, not a design.
